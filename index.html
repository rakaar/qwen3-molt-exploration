Small interventions.

What happens when we change one component inside a language model? Four experiments with Qwen3-4B and MOLTs—sparse, gated low-rank transforms—from answer steering to knowing when to abstain.

01Answer steering · Layer 22

From “large”
to “cold.”

Ask for the opposite of “small” and the model answers “large.” Subtract a small-associated MOLT contribution and add a hot-associated one: the same prompt now yields “cold,” with 99.8653% next-token probability.

Active alternative pairs fail to reproduce the flip, including controls matched to the selected edit’s magnitude.

A result on one prompt pair, with the native MLP retained and attention unfrozen. It does not establish general semantic replacement.

02Cross-prompt steering · Layer 3

Spider → ant.
Eight → six.

“How many legs does the animal that weaves webs have?” Normally, the answer is 8. Patching one MOLT contribution from an explicit ant question changes the generated answer to 6.

At strength 32, the next-token probability of 6 reaches 99.999976%. A gate-only control also achieves the answer flip.

This shows answer steering; it does not establish that an internal spider representation was replaced by an ant representation.

03Passage grounding · Layer 19

From a made-up answer
to “I don’t know.”

When a passage does not support an answer, can an intervention make the model withhold it? Adding the input-dependent output of one MOLT turns unsupported answers into coherent, passage-grounded abstentions in 7 of 10 selected cases.

The report pairs natural activation evidence with the exact intervention, complete responses, and strength sweeps.

Ten selected baseline failures, with strengths chosen per question. This is an exploratory result, not a model-wide success rate.

04Data privacy & authorization · Layer 11

A privacy-associated
transform. A refusal.

The native model follows the access policy: share a synthetic API key when authorized; refuse otherwise. Fixing one MOLT’s estimated gate to 16 makes it refuse even authorized requests, in all three tested service variants.

The report follows natural activation contrasts through to the causal intervention and full model responses.

Induced over-refusal on three variants of one template. This is not evidence of improved privacy protection or a general authorization detector.

Behind the experiments

Changing behavior.
Investigating mechanisms.

A MOLT contributes a scalar gate times an input-dependent low-rank output. These experiments modify selected contributions while retaining the native model computation. The four visuals above are summary diagrams; the linked reports contain measured figures, exact prompts, methods, and controls.

These are exploratory, prompt-specific results. Labels suggest hypotheses; activation alone does not establish a causal mechanism. Browse the earlier research notebook or inspect the code and evidence on GitHub.
