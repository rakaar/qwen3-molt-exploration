The same one-token intervention

In each raw text-completion prompt, the gate is zero on every earlier input token. It turns on at the final is, where the model predicts the first answer token. We set that gate to zero by subtracting this MOLT’s contribution from the native MLP output.

What does P(answer) mean?

Each column scores its own displayed answer under its own condition. P(answer) is the conditional probability of the exact answer-token sequence, including its leading space and lowercase spelling. It excludes the punctuation or end token that follows, and does not sum over alternative capitalization or formatting.

The month and letter answers are each one token. The edited Roman answer xii is two tokens: x and ii. Its probability is P( x | prompt, edit) × P(ii | prompt + x, edit). The probability shown is for the complete two-token answer, not just its first prefix.

Initial answers, not full-continuation accuracy. These raw completions continue beyond the answer and can repeat or drift. The full recorded continuations are available under each example. No generated tokens were edited.

Does the contribution align across five languages?

Ask for the eleventh month of the year in English, Spanish, Chinese, French, and Hindi. Each 5×5 matrix compares the full native MOLT contribution c(x) = φ(x) × UVx at the final input token in two prompt contexts. French and Hindi have no active input position in those particular raw prefixes, so their full-contribution cosine is undefined.

Which tokens are compared, and what does this establish?

The position rule is fixed: use the final input token in every case. Raw English, Spanish, and Chinese fire at that position. Raw French and Hindi are zero throughout the input, so there is no earlier active token to substitute. In chat, all five final tokens are assistant-prefix \n\n tokens, before any answer is generated. The exact zero-based position, token ID, gate, and vector norm are listed for each prompt below.

The matrices compare original positive, full gate-times-operator vectors in native MLP-output units. They retain the checkpoint’s input/output standardizers and use no final RMSNorm or vocabulary projection. Cosines are calculated in FP64 from saved FP32 vectors. A positive scalar gate affects magnitude, not direction. A zero gate gives a zero contribution and undefined cosine, including its diagonal.

All five chat answers name November correctly. The raw French continuation incorrectly says December; the raw Hindi continuation does not directly answer the month. These inactive cases remain visible rather than being treated as successful native-capability controls. The chat and raw versions differ in wording and format, so this comparison does not isolate either factor.

These are geometric observations, without a new causal intervention or cross-language patch. The shared chat-prefix tokens may contribute to the alignment. This study does not establish that the aligned direction is unique to eleven or that it causes the same answer change in each language.

05

Is this specific to the eleventh?

Keep each successful English raw-prefix template fixed and change only the ordinal: 11th, 1st, 2nd, 4th, and 5th. Every comparison below is with that domain’s eleventh prompt, at the same final input token is. These are new native observations; no gate is manipulated in this check.

The gate distinguishes these ordinals more than the operator direction does. It is active for eleventh and zero for all eight other-ordinal controls. Their full contribution c(x) is therefore zero, so its cosine is undefined, not zero. The separate ungated operator UVx still has a measurable direction: Roman controls have cosine 0.9426–0.9878 with the eleventh operator.

In this small set, activation is selective for the eleventh wording, while high operator alignment is not exclusive to eleven. This does not establish general ordinal selectivity or explain why removing the eleventh contribution makes twelve win.

What this establishes

On this prompt, removing T1181 reverses the initial-answer flip caused by T2375 removal. T1181 helps sustain December’s advantage when T2375 is absent, while substantial December probability remains with both off. These interventions do not establish a universal ordinal mechanism or a direct connection between the two MOLTs.

The full MOLT magnitudes differ

Magnitude means Euclidean (L2) length. We retain the learned MOLT input/output standardizers. There is no extra RMSNorm applied to these contribution vectors.

PromptGate φ(x)Operator ‖UVx‖₂Full MOLT ‖c(x)‖₂

Gate φ(x)

A nonnegative scalar, including the checkpoint’s learned thresholding. It controls how strongly this operator contributes.

Operator UVx

The input-dependent vector, with checkpoint output scaling included to express it in native MLP-output units.

Full MOLT contribution

c(x) = φ(x) × UVx. Its L2 length is approximately φ(x) × ‖UVx‖₂, up to floating-point rounding.

09

What this result establishes

Observed

  • At these three prompts’ final is token, L29–T2375 has a nonzero gate.
  • Removing only its contribution at that position changes the initial answer from the eleventh to the twelfth item.
  • The three contributions have similar directions and different magnitudes.

Still open

  • Why twelve wins after removal.
  • Whether the shared direction or its smaller prompt-specific remainder is causally important.
  • How much the effect depends on the shared “eleventh” wording and on later model computations.

The MOLT gate/operator calculation uses the checkpoint’s input standardization and output rescaling:

z = (residual − input_mean) / input_std
 operator = (z @ V @ U) × output_std
 contribution = gate(z) × operator

This is distinct from the model’s final RMSNorm. Final RMSNorm and the output matrix were used in separate vocabulary-readout analyses; neither is applied to the cosine comparison on this page.

Native and zero-strength runs matched exactly in the source study. Saved vector audits checked gates, operators, intervention masks, subtraction, and BF16 rounding. Later native passes reproduced the saved MLP outputs exactly. The displayed vectors are the intended positive MOLT contributions before the BF16 subtraction rounding; the actual edit is their negative, followed by one BF16 cast.

The answer probabilities and full continuations come from the previously audited raw-prefix runs. The original examples and multilingual matrix use saved records.
