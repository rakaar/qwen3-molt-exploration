The adapter is longer in 48/50 matched pairs, tied in two, shorter in none. Mean length increases on all ten scenarios. Both models use identical prompts, sampling settings, token caps and disabled thinking; every answer ends normally.

What might explain it?

All 6,000 training answers were prose, 20–99 words each, median 49; none requested or answered solely A/B. A plausible explanation is that fine-tuning strengthens this prose-answering habit while weakening adherence to short-output instructions. LIMA provides general evidence that supervised fine-tuning can teach response formats, but does not establish the mechanism in this adapter. We have not isolated a Lion-specific effect.

Verbosity itself is not evidence of misalignment: the adapter’s 25 preferred answers average 18.56 words, versus 29.06 for its 18 undesired answers. We observe both a format/style change and more undesired choices; we have not established that one causes the other. A future constrained A/B or option-probability evaluation could remove the length difference from the choice measurement. No such follow-up has been run.
