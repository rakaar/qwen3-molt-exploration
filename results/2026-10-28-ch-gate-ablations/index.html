Turning off CH gates.

We removed five CH-related transform contributions, one at a time, while Qwen3-4B answered few-shot spelling questions. Confidence sometimes changed. Every greedy response stayed the same.

These are 12 unique prompts reused across five single-feature interventions, not 60 independent questions. No joint gate ablation was performed.

The clearest effect

Less confidence.
The same letters.

For the lowercase chair prompt, removing L3–T2334 lowered next-token P(ch) from 70.646% to 45.779% (−24.867 percentage points). The response remained ch.

Removing L29–T2334 separately lowered it to 67.334%. Uppercase cheek moved in the other direction: P(CH) increased under either of these removals.

When a gate is already off

All five selected CH gates were inactive on the input child token, in both formats. Those ten interventions removed zero contribution and preserved probabilities exactly.

This is not a causal demonstration of absorption. The follow-up tests these five candidates only. The earlier broader investigation includes other transforms, such as L28–T1123.

What was changed

One estimated contribution, at the input word.

  1. Keep native Qwen computation. Observe the residual after the selected layer’s attention and before post_attention_layernorm. Standardize it with the released MOLT statistics.
  2. Measure the natural gate. The encoder preactivation is retained only when it is positive and above the released threshold.
  3. Remove that feature’s estimated contribution. At every input token overlapping the target word, subtract gate × ((z @ V) @ U) × output_std from that layer’s native raw MLP output. Compute the edit in FP32, then cast once to BF16.
  4. Let downstream computation run normally. No other MLP position is edited at the intervention boundary. Attention remains unfrozen. Generated tokens and their gate activity are not edited.

This preserves the native MLP reconstruction remainder. It is estimated-component subtraction, not complete replacement of a native MLP with a MOLT reconstruction.

Controls, precision, and verification
  • Unhooked native generation, a zero-strength hook control, gate-off free generation, and gate-off scoring with the fixed native continuation.
  • All 60 zero-strength controls reproduce native token IDs and recorded probabilities exactly. The later four-gate study also exactly reproduces the preceding L3 study’s native baselines.
  • 120 saved vector files independently reconstructed on CPU: encoder scores, thresholding, low-rank outputs, intended edits, and actual BF16-rounded edits. All checks passed.
  • Native weights: BF16. MOLT computation: FP32. TF32 disabled. Eager attention, thinking disabled, greedy decoding, maximum 16 new tokens. Hooks removed after every condition and after each study.

Interpretation

What these results establish.

Observed in this pilot

The estimated L3–T2334 contribution affects confidence on some prompts, especially lowercase chair. Other effects are small or mixed. Every tested single-gate ablation preserves the native greedy response.

L4–T153 and L32–T1516 were inactive on every prompt. L34–T929 was active only on the che subtoken of cheek. Its answer-slot changes were below 0.004 percentage points.

Still unresolved

This does not establish that CH information is absent, that these gates are generally unnecessary, or that another feature absorbs their function. No joint ablation or active norm-matched specificity control was run in this follow-up.

The 12 prompts are a small fixed set. Labels selected the candidates, full-layer reconstruction fidelity was not evaluated here, and the scan was not exhaustive. See the earlier investigation for its distinct all-layer screen and additional candidates.
