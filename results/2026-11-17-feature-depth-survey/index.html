01 / THE QUESTION

From punctuation
to meaning?

The starting intuition comes from vision models: early features can describe simple visual patterns, while deeper features combine them into richer structures. We asked a narrower question here: do descriptions of language-model features become more semantic with depth?

A MOLT is a sparse, gated low-rank transform. A single-layer transcoder approximates an MLP with a sparse set of features. Both come with automatically generated descriptions of activation patterns. We classified those descriptions, without running new activation or steering experiments.

Starting hypothesis
Surface form→Grammar→Semantic meaning
A useful hypothesis to test; not a guaranteed ordering of transformer layers.

Layer 0 is the first transformer block. Feature IDs from the two methods are not matched concepts.

01

Semantic descriptions occur early.

Both samples include semantic and abstract-sounding descriptions in early layers. Their presence alone does not establish a general concept detector.

02

Depth is not a steady increase.

The abstract / relational fraction is highest in the middle group for both methods, then lower in the late group. Individual layers are noisier.

03

Description styles differ sharply.

Transcoder labels tend to name topics. MOLT explanations contain more detail about tokens, grammatical context and relations. That can change their categories.

03 / WHAT WE COUNTED

Five categories.
Careful boundaries.

The unit is the complete available title and description. The defining target determines the category; abstract-sounding language in a title is not sufficient.

06 / THE STUDY DESIGN

Random samples.
Blinded layers.

No GPU experiments were run. This study measures descriptions of features.

01

Freeze the catalog and rubric.

The MOLT catalog revision, source-file hashes and five-category rubric were fixed. The transcoder survey copied the rubric byte for byte. Published Neuronpedia explanation batches were cached and hash-verified.

02

Sample within every layer.

Each source contributes two calibration and 40 separate main descriptions per layer. MOLT features were sampled uniformly among available explanations. A batch-slot sampler gives every nonempty published transcoder explanation record equal sampling probability.

03

Hide depth from the annotators.

Luna raters saw opaque random IDs and complete available text. Layer, feature ID, rank, activity and quality scores were hidden. Primary assignments were shuffled across all layers.

04

Measure agreement and retain uncertainty.

Two main items per layer were rated by all three streams: 72 shared items per source. Shared items use majority voting; three-way disagreement becomes mixed/unclear. Other items have a single primary rating.

Sampling details, missing labels and the annotation audit ＋

Exactly what was sampled?

MOLT sampling seed: 2026111701. Transcoder seed: 2026111702. Both sample without replacement. Complete transcoder batch-key lists were frozen; 256-slot frames correct for unequal batch lengths by rejecting empty padding slots. Selected feature IDs are unique within every layer.

The global uniqueness of all published transcoder explanation records was not audited. A check of 365,718 records in downloaded batches found no duplicate feature IDs within layers; duplicates elsewhere could still give a feature extra weight.

What about missing descriptions?

The fractions are conditional on available descriptions. MOLT layer 10 has 1,530 of 2,480 slots explained; layer 22 has 1,203 of 2,480. The exact transcoder label-population coverage was not measured. Missing labels could change a full-population depth curve.

What was corrected?

Two initial transcoder annotation streams used bulk category sequences and copied or generic reasons; one confirmed assignment errors. Those complete streams were discarded and repeated by fresh Luna raters in small batches with explicit per-ID decisions. The sample and rubric were unchanged. The website uses only final checked annotations.

Read the annotation audit ↗

What do the error bars mean?

Approximate Wilson 95% intervals reflect finite sampling of available descriptions. They exclude systematic label errors, annotation errors and source-style bias. They omit a small finite-population correction and are not simultaneous intervals across all layers.

07 / WHAT THE RESULTS CAN SUPPORT

The descriptions are different.
So is the evidence.

Using one rubric makes the annotation procedure consistent. It does not make the two sources equivalent measurements of abstraction.

A short topic label and a detailed explanation offer different information to a classifier. Text length, explanation model, prompting and corpus can all influence the fractions.

01

“Abstract” is an operational category.

It includes abstract concepts, quantities, relations and communicative functions. It is not a direct measurement of compositional complexity or a universal abstraction scale.

02

A concept word can be a lexical cue.

A feature labelled “justice” could respond to the literal token or an occupational title. Paraphrases and contrasting meanings are needed to distinguish those possibilities.

03

The evidence comes from different protocols.

MOLT descriptions use UltraChat evidence. The transcoder labels were generated with Gemini-2.0-Flash from Pile-derived examples. The MOLT release names a different explanation and scoring model. Those differences can change what gets described.

04

This is not a test of causal function.

No new activation, ablation or steering tests were performed for this survey. Category fractions cannot establish what a feature computes or whether either method produces better features.
