Does the selected MOLT pair outperform random MOLTs?

Results

The selected L22–T2339/L22–T941 pair reaches 99.8653% P(cold). Exact replacement with the donor’s complete MLP output gives 7.00034e-10% P(cold) and 94.1706% P(large).

None of the 3 gate-active alternative pairs reaches 99% P(cold) at this fixed strength. This small set does not establish general semantic selectivity.

418 measured conditions, 60 matched batches with clean identity rows, and independently recomputed full-vocabulary probabilities. All uniform samples, including zero edits, are retained.

What the controls show

All 100 uniformly sampled MOLTs were inactive at both source tokens. Their individual and paired edits therefore leave the model unchanged. This is a weak null control by itself.

We also tested all other available gate-active individuals and all three distinct active pairs. None makes cold the top token. The pair −4.5 receiver T8 +4.5 donor T71 has correction norm 63.0534, versus 65.3121 for the selected pair, but still gives effectively zero P(cold). This provides a more informative comparison at a similar, though not exactly matched, magnitude.

Neither selected hot-only addition nor selected small-only subtraction reaches 99% P(cold). The successful condition uses both contributions.

Magnitude and interpretation

Equal coefficients do not imply equal correction norms. Ranks, gate magnitudes, and low-rank outputs differ. These random-MOLT controls are not norm-matched random-direction controls. Uniform inactive cases cannot establish semantic selectivity; the small active set and this single prompt pair limit the conclusion. Whole-MLP replacement at strength 1 also differs in magnitude and direction from the extrapolated selected MOLT edit at strength 4.5.

P(cold) is the full-vocabulary next-token probability for lowercase token 87072; P(large) uses token 16767. Uppercase variants are separate tokens. The probabilities are not renormalized over two answers.
