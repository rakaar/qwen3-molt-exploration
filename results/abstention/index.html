Abstention experiments.

What makes Qwen3-4B say “I don’t know”? One place for the steering experiments, native gate heatmaps, confidence searches and negative results.

Start with the completed comparison.

20 questions × input-only, output-only and input + output steering →

For the original narrative and posters: the first ten made-up-answer cases.

Reading the results: “Made up an answer” means the passage does not support the answer; it can still be true external knowledge. Clear abstention means a coherent “I don’t know / it’s not in the passage” without giving the substantive answer. Inducing this on an answer-present question is false abstention. The cohorts overlap across reports and must not be counted as independent replications.

Most later L19–T368 experiments add a × low_rank_output(current_input) to the native MLP output; the early fixed-gate experiment instead replaces the estimated gate. Strengths were found with sweeps and follow-ups, not a guaranteed binary search or continuous minimum. The confidence and uncertainty studies below are observational; no useful pre-answer confidence gate or confidence–strength correlation has been established.
